Data Sources
Built on Three Public Aviation Datasets

The framework synthesizes three complementary datasets to connect simulated engine physics with real-world operational outcomes.

🛸
NASA N-CMAPSS
Engine Physics
High-fidelity turbofan degradation simulation with 14 physical sensors across 6.5M flight records. Provides ground-truth RUL labels for model training.
✈️
FAA Service Difficulty Reports
Maintenance Events
1,533 real engine SDR events (JASC 7200, 2020–2024), manually pulled from sdrs.faa.gov — used to validate that engine failures are a substantial, persistent share of U.S. carrier maintenance events.
📊
Bureau of Transportation Statistics
Flight Operations
21,073 annual carrier-caused cancellations across 14 U.S. carriers — used as the baseline against which avoidable, engine-related groundings are estimated.
Methodology
Four-Stage Open-Source Pipeline

From raw sensor telemetry to actionable maintenance recommendations — designed to run on standard hardware.

1
Data Ingestion
NASA HDF5 archives downloaded & validated; 5 years of FAA SDR engine records and BTS 2025 carrier cancellations parsed.
Complete
2
Feature Engineering
Per-cycle sensor aggregates plus per-unit trend signal — rolling means, short-horizon deltas, and drift from each engine's first recorded cycle.
Complete
3
RUL Prediction
XGBoost regressor, leave-one-unit-out cross-validated. 9.56-cycle mean CV RMSE (DS02), 7.78 (DS01).
Complete
4
Operational Validation
RUL detection rate × engine fraction × BTS baseline, cross-checked with a sensitivity analysis across the underlying assumptions.

About
Open-Source Research for U.S. Aviation

This study was launched in March 2026 by Arnav Prashant Joshi, a researcher specializing in data synthesis and optimization for complex operational systems. The goal is to close a long-standing gap: predictive engine maintenance has historically required costly enterprise systems, placing it out of reach for the small regional carriers critical to the U.S. aviation network.

All code, pipelines, and model artifacts will be publicly released upon completion. The framework is designed to run on standard hardware — no specialized infrastructure required — ensuring data-driven maintenance is accessible across the entire U.S. aviation system.

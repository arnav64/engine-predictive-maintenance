Predictive Maintenance Framework for Aircraft Engines

An open-source Remaining Useful Life prediction system that makes data-driven engine maintenance accessible to every U.S. aviation operator — including small regional carriers.

Mission

Reducing Unplanned Aircraft Groundings Nationwide

Unplanned aircraft groundings have reached record levels across the United States. A core driver is that predictive engine maintenance has historically required costly enterprise systems — placing it out of reach for the small regional carriers that are critical to the U.S. aviation network.

This study synthesizes NASA engine performance data, FAA Service Difficulty Reports, and Bureau of Transportation Statistics records to model the Remaining Useful Life (RUL) of aircraft engines. Predictions are validated against real U.S. flight network data to quantify their operational impact.

Because this framework is open-source and publicly available, it is nationally scalable — not proprietary to any single organization — ensuring that data-driven predictive maintenance capabilities are accessible across the entire U.S. aviation system.

Data Sources

Built on Public Aviation Data

The framework synthesizes three complementary public datasets to connect simulated engine physics with real-world operational outcomes.

🛸
NASA N-CMAPSS
Engine Physics
Commercial Modular Aero-Propulsion System Simulation — high-fidelity turbofan degradation data with 14 physical sensors (temperatures, pressures, speeds, fuel flow) across 6.5 million flight records. Provides ground-truth RUL labels for model training.
✈️
FAA Service Difficulty Reports
Maintenance Events
Reported mechanical problems and unscheduled maintenance events from U.S. operators — used to validate model predictions against real-world failure patterns and cross-reference ground incidents.
📊
Bureau of Transportation Statistics
Flight Operations
Comprehensive U.S. flight schedule, delay, and cancellation records — used to quantify the downstream operational and financial cost of unplanned groundings across the national aviation network.
Methodology

Four-Stage Open-Source Pipeline

From raw sensor telemetry to actionable maintenance recommendations — a fully reproducible, open-source pipeline designed to run on standard hardware.

1

Download and validate NASA N-CMAPSS HDF5 archives; parse FAA SDR and BTS flight records.

About

Active Open-Source Research

This study was launched in March 2026 by Arnav Prashant Joshi, a researcher specializing in data synthesis and optimization for complex operational systems. The predictive maintenance framework is being developed as a fully open-source contribution to U.S. aviation infrastructure.

The work is advised by Alain Berube of AerConsul LLC — who brings over 30 years of aerospace and defense leadership, including service as CEO of MTU Aero Engines North America and senior executive roles at four additional international engine maintenance organizations.

All code, data pipelines, and model artifacts will be publicly released upon project completion. The framework is designed to run on standard hardware without specialized enterprise infrastructure, ensuring accessibility for small and regional carriers across the U.S. aviation network.
